\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

\textbf{Accuracy.} We score with the Brier score~\citep{brier1950},
$\mathrm{BS}(p) = n^{-1}\sum(p_i - y_i)^2$, and compare a model with the crowd
by the \emph{Brier edge} $\Delta = \mathrm{BS}(q) - \mathrm{BS}(p)$, so positive
means the model was more accurate. We report the difference rather than two
levels because the level depends on how hard a window's questions happened to be.

\textbf{Splitting the score.} We separate the Brier score with Murphy's
partition~\citep{murphy1973},
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{BS} \;=\; \underbrace{\mathrm{REL}}_{\text{calibration error}}
\;-\; \underbrace{\mathrm{RES}}_{\text{sorting}}
\;+\; \underbrace{\mathrm{UNC}}_{\text{fixed by outcomes}},
\label{eq:murphy}
\end{equation}
computed over ten equal-width bins. Reliability measures whether stated
probabilities are honest; resolution measures whether the forecaster separates
events that happen from events that do not. Uncertainty depends only on the
outcomes, so it cancels from the edge.

\textbf{Recalibration control.} If the gap were a calibration failure it could be
repaired after the fact by rescaling. We test that directly, fitting the
two-parameter Platt sigmoid~\citep{platt1999} to each model's probabilities on the
calibration split and scoring the mapped probabilities on the test split. We
report the fraction of the Brier gap it recovers.

\textbf{Money.} We simulate trading from the book at the forecast moment: if the
model's probability is above the best ask we buy Yes, if below the best bid we
buy No, otherwise no trade. Each venue's real taker fee is charged. Rows with a
one-sided or crossed book, or a spread wider than 0.50, are never traded. Returns
are per dollar staked.

\textbf{Direction.} A model \emph{leans with the crowd} on a row when its
probability sits further toward the side the market already favours than the
market price does, and \emph{against} the crowd when it sits on the far side of
the price from that side. A model exactly at the price, or any model on a row the
market prices at exactly 0.5, leans neither way, which is why the two shares do
not sum to one. A model that says 0.60 when the market says 0.70 counts as leaning against
the crowd, even though both favour Yes; the model is less confident, not
contradicting.

\textbf{News value.} The value of news is the Brier score without it minus the
Brier score with it, so positive means the news helped. A forecast moved
\emph{toward} the market when the with-news answer is closer to the price;
movements smaller than 0.02 count as neither toward nor away.

\textbf{Correlated errors.} Raw error correlation overstates shared judgment when
forecasts move little relative to outcomes, so the primary measure correlates
\emph{departures from the market price}, $p-q$; because two forecasters that
shrink toward the same base rate agree on departures regardless, we fit each
model's own shrinkage and correlate the residuals. The null is simulated
forecasters carrying each model's fitted shrinkage but independent noise.
\emph{Wrong together} is the share of model pairs on the same incorrect side of
even money, compared with the independent rate. Equations and construction
are in Appendix~\ref{app:correlmethod}.

\textbf{Exploratory status.} The analyses in this paper, including the scripts
of Appendix~\ref{app:exploratory}, are exploratory.

\textbf{Depth.} We measure whether losses grow as the book thins by the
thin-minus-deep return gap and the slope of return on the natural log of depth.

\textbf{Uncertainty.} All intervals are 95 per cent bootstrap ranges resampling
\emph{events} rather than rows, because markets within one event move together
and resampling rows would give intervals that are too narrow. The main tables use
2{,}000 resamples; exploratory scripts use 1{,}000.

\textbf{Which rows a number uses.} Every result is computed on the test split
or a named subset of it. The exceptions are Table~\ref{tab:benchmark}, which
describes the benchmark, counts over whole runs (failures, probe flag rates), and
the cross-fitted variants below. The calibration split is used to fit the recalibration
control and the combination rules, which are then scored on the test split; the
cross-fitted variants score every row because no held-out set
remains (Figure~\ref{fig:recalibration}). Unless a number is explicitly about
news it is the all-rows arm.

\textbf{Gates and quarantine.} A calibration gate on model-window-bucket cells
(Appendix~\ref{app:gate}) and the memory-probe quarantine
(Appendix~\ref{app:memory}) apply only to the convergence test of
Appendix~\ref{app:convergence}. Every other number, Table~\ref{tab:crowd}
included, uses all rows, flagged ones too.
