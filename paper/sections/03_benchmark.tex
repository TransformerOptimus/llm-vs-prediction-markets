\section{Benchmark}
\label{sec:benchmark}

\textbf{A row is a market-moment.} One row is one market at one instant. The
model-visible part is the question, event title, resolution rules, scheduled close
and a frozen news snapshot. The analysis-only part (order book, price, outcome,
price path, fee schedule) is never shown to any model; the separation is enforced
in the harness.

\textbf{Three windows, never pooled.} \winA is \polybench's February 2026 week on
\polymarket~\citep{cheng2026polybench}, with every model asked under our protocol. \winC is
\kalshi over four weeks in May--June 2026, and \winB is \polymarket over 26
days in July--August 2026 (Table~\ref{tab:benchmark}). The letters are labels, not calendar order.

\textbf{Sampling.} Each market enters at most once, on the first day its 18:00
UTC order book is live: quotes on both sides, spread ten cents or less. Each day contributes 120
non-ladder rows (24 from each of five per-day depth groups, not the analysis
buckets of Table~\ref{tab:benchmark}) plus 20 ladder rows; one \winB day had
only 99 eligible non-ladder markets.
Ladders (\emph{Bitcoin above \$70k, \$72k, \$74k}) stay out of the primary set and are scored on their own in
Section~\ref{sec:results-market}.
\winA was drawn without the live-book rule; we use its rows as \polybench
published them.

\textbf{The market's probability.} We take the crowd's probability to be the
midpoint of the best bid and best ask at the forecast moment. \winB and \winC
store that midpoint directly. On \winA the stored price is a stale sweep, so we
recompute the midpoint from the book for consistency across windows.

\textbf{News.} For \winB and \winC we built a frozen snapshot from the Common
Crawl news archive, captured strictly before the forecast moment; rows whose
news carries a late dateline are dropped. \winA keeps the articles \polybench
supplied, stamped as fetched at the forecast moment; 159 of its rows carry a
dateline at least two days after that stamp, so those articles were fetched
later, and the rows are dropped. About seven in ten \winB and \winC rows have no
news at all, so results distinguish two arms: rows with news and all rows. The attachment rules are in
Appendix~\ref{app:newsattach}.

\textbf{The primary analysis set.} Headline numbers use a filtered subset.
Ladder rows go because their prices are formulas. Rows whose end date appears to
have moved go because the model may have answered a different question from the
one that settled; detection relies on internal inconsistency (slug date versus
game start, or scheduled close versus a date in the question text), so a clean
edit that left no trace would pass (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). This fires
on 253 \winB rows and none elsewhere. Late-dateline rows go for the leakage
reason above (159 of 2{,}997 on \winA). Rows priced outside 0.05 to 0.95 go
because near the boundaries one surprise swamps the comparison. What remains is
1{,}550, 1{,}865 and 2{,}669 rows.

\textbf{Split.} Each window's primary set is divided 20 per cent calibration and
80 per cent test, drawn within each window-by-depth-bucket-by-sports cell so the
two halves match in composition, under a fixed seed. The recalibration control of Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} is fitted on the
calibration split, so it never sees the rows it is judged on.

\textbf{Depth.} Depth is the dollars resting on the five best prices on each side
of the Yes book at the forecast moment. Each window's primary set is cut into
five equal-count buckets (Table~\ref{tab:benchmark}), formed within a window, so
bucket~1 always means thin relative to that window.

\textbf{Fees.} Trading results charge each venue's real taker-fee schedule per
row; both schedules peak at even money, which is where most disputed trades sit.
The exact formulas are in Appendix~\ref{app:feeformula}.
